Some decisions are too important to make in the noise.

01

The process is being handled. You are the part nobody is looking after.

Something happens when a personal situation acquires a legal or financial process. The two stop being separable. A question about a house becomes a question about the last twenty years. A decision that should take an afternoon takes four months, because it is not really the decision you are stuck on.

Meanwhile everyone has an opinion. Your friends mean well. Your family means well. But their advice comes shaped by their own history, their own fears, their own stake in what you decide. None of it is neutral, and in the middle of it your own judgement becomes very hard to hear.

So you go round the same three arguments with yourself at four in the morning. You defer the thing you know needs deciding. You take advice that is sound and cannot act on it. None of that is a failure of intelligence. It is what proximity does.

You are almost certainly capable of this decision. You are simply too close to it to see it clearly.

02

I help you separate what matters from what is merely loud.

We work through what you are actually facing. What is fact and what is fear. What is yours to decide and what is not. Which of the voices around you is worth weight, and which one you have simply been carrying the longest.

I ask better questions than the ones you have been asking yourself, and I listen more carefully than you are probably used to. I notice the places where your reasoning turns, where you argue against your own interest, where a position you are defending is not one you actually hold.

Most of it comes back to two things. What is genuinely important, as against what has become a point of principle. And what any of it will look like in the years that follow, because a situation is rarely the whole of a life and that is difficult to hold on to from inside one.

I show you what you are already doing, so you can see it and decide differently if you choose to.

Where this applies

Succession and inheritance. The sale or handover of a business. Ageing parents and the decisions that come with them. Family estates. Divorce and separation. Any decision whose consequences are both financial and personal.

What it is

A confidential hour with someone independent, who understands the process you are in and has no interest in the outcome beyond yours. Clearer ground before the next meeting, the next letter, the next conversation you have been putting off.

What it is not

Not legal advice. Not therapy. Not mediation, and not a message carried between parties. I do not sit in your negotiations or speak to the other side. Your advisers advise. I work with the person receiving the advice.

Your solicitor handles the process. I work with the person at the centre of it.

Advice tells you what your options are and what each one costs. It cannot tell you which one you will be able to live with, and it is not designed to. That question is yours, and it is usually the harder one.

I work alongside your advisers, not instead of them. Nothing you say to me changes your legal position, and nothing I do requires anyone else's involvement. Most clients simply arrive at their next appointment already knowing what they want.

03

I spent twelve years advising people at one of the most difficult times in their lives. Then I found myself at the worst point in mine.

I spent my career as a medical negligence solicitor, latterly as a partner at one of Manchester's most respected firms, in practices recognised by Legal 500 and Chambers and Partners. My work was rarely about compensation. It was about people who had been failed wanting accountability, and the truth, and to be certain it would not happen to somebody else. Then twenty years in FCA-regulated businesses and property investment and development, where the risk was mine to hold, not a client's.

By any professional measure I was well equipped for what happened to me in 2019. I could read a settlement, model an outcome, hold my nerve in a room full of people arguing. None of it helped.

That is the part I did not expect, and the part I now understand better than anything else. Competence and clarity are not the same thing. You can hold every relevant fact and still be unable to see the shape of your own situation, because you are standing inside it.

What I needed then did not exist in any form I could find. Not a solicitor, I had those. Not a therapist. Someone intelligent and entirely independent, who understood the machinery and could help me think straight while it was happening.

Twelve years in practice. Medical negligence, latterly as partner. Legal 500 and Chambers recognised firms.

Entirely confidential. Nothing discussed leaves the room, and nothing is reported to anyone, including whoever made the introduction.

04

Your client understands your advice. Acting on it is the other problem.

You will recognise the client. Sound instructions, sensible options, and a decision that keeps not being made. They are trying to think straight while carrying a weight that has nothing to do with the process itself. It arrives in your file as changed instructions, unreturned calls, positions taken and abandoned, and a matter that costs more and takes longer than it needed to.

I give that client somewhere else to do the thinking. They arrive at your meetings having already worked out what they want, which tends to make your job easier and their experience of the process considerably better.

I do not advise on law, comment on strategy, or contact the other side. I do not sit in your meetings unless you ask me to. I am comfortable being introduced and then staying out of the way.
